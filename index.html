Lunar Contactless Power Transfer Physics & State Observer Model

High-frequency (85 kHz) contactless Inductive Power Transfer (IPT) enables surface mobile assets—such as pressurized rovers, autonomous excavation drills, and ISRU oxygen extraction plants—to tap high-voltage lunar transmission spines without hazardous physical regolith-fouled copper-mating connectors. Physical shifts in mechanical coupling fundamentally alter the complex equivalent impedance $Z_{\text{in}}$, necessitating instantaneous closed-loop phase cancellation.

1. Mutual Coupling & Reflected Impedance

Neumann & Kirchhoff

The primary backbone transmission conductor induces an open-circuit voltage in the secondary split-core ferritic pickup tap. The total impedance reflected back into the primary trunk conductor $Z_{\text{ref}}$ is a non-linear function of mutual inductance $M$, angular drive frequency $\omega$, and secondary secondary impedance $Z_2$:

$$M = k \sqrt{L_1 L_2} \approx \frac{\mu_0 \mu_r N_1 N_2 A_{\text{core}}}{l_{\text{gap}} + \frac{l_{\text{core}}}{\mu_r}}$$
$$Z_{\text{in}}(\omega) = j\omega L_1 + R_1 + \frac{\omega^2 M^2}{R_2 + j\left(\omega L_2 - \frac{1}{\omega C_{\text{matrix}}}\right) + Z_{\text{load}}}$$

When gap $l_{\text{gap}}$ dilates from 1 mm to 8 mm, mutual inductance $M$ drops parabolically, causing reflected reactance to vanish and generating massive reactive circulating current in the primary backbone.

2. Phase Shift & Tank Power Factor

Vector Power Analysis

Maximum active power transfer and minimal inverter bridge switching losses occur strictly when the phase angle $\theta = \arg(Z_{\text{in}})$ equals zero (unity power factor, $\cos\theta = 1.0$). Reactive power surge $Q_{\text{reactive}}$ creates destructive $I^2R$ heating in the cryogenic or lunar diurnal thermal vacuum:

$$\theta = \arctan\left(\frac{\text{Im}[Z_{\text{in}}]}{\text{Re}[Z_{\text{in}}]}\right) = \arctan\left(\frac{\omega L_1 - \frac{\omega^3 M^2 X_2}{|Z_2|^2}}{R_1 + \frac{\omega^2 M^2 R_2}{|Z_2|^2}}\right)$$
$$P_{\text{active}} = V_{\text{rms}} I_{\text{rms}} \cos(\theta), \quad Q_{\text{reactive}} = V_{\text{rms}} I_{\text{rms}} \sin(\theta)$$

A phase drift of merely $\theta = \pm 35^\circ$ increases reactive voltamperes by $57\%$, which triggers overcurrent trip faults in the upstream central fission surface power (FSP) inverter.

3. Discrete Kalman State Inversion

Observer Equation

To avoid heavy and failure-prone high-frequency current sensors on every tap segment, our software deploys an Extended Kalman Filter (EKF) observer observing input clamp voltage $v_1(t)$ and primary current differential $\Delta i_1(t)$ to invert physical coupling $k(t)$ and load resistance $R_L$:

$$\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{k+1} = \mathbf{A} \hat{\mathbf{x}}_k + \mathbf{B} \mathbf{u}_k + \mathbf{K}_k \left(\mathbf{y}_k - \mathbf{C} \hat{\mathbf{x}}_k\right), \quad \mathbf{x} = \begin{bmatrix} M(t) \\ C_{\text{eff}}(t) \\ R_{\text{load}}(t) \end{bmatrix}$$
$$\Delta C_{\text{correction}} = \frac{1}{\omega^2 \left(L_2 - \frac{M^2}{L_1 + X_{\text{src}}/\omega}\right)} - C_{\text{current}}$$

The observer solves in under $4.2\ \mu\text{s}$ on a rad-hard FPGA, enabling zero-voltage-switching (ZVS) survival across fast physical vibrations.

4. Binary Switched Compensation Bank

GaN HEMT Matrix

Compensation is delivered via a 6-bit binary-weighted capacitor network governed by Gallium Nitride (GaN) bidirectionally blocking switches. The system switches exclusively at zero-current crossings to prevent transient arcs in low-pressure vacuum:

$$C_{\text{matrix}} = C_0 + \sum_{n=0}^{5} b_n \cdot \left(2^n C_{\text{base}}\right), \quad b_n \in \{0, 1\}$$
$$P_{\text{loss, thermal}} = I_{\text{sec}}^2 \cdot \left(\text{ESR}_{\text{eff}} + R_{\text{coil}}(T)\right), \quad R(T) = R_0(1 + \alpha(T - T_0))$$

Maintains $Q > 150$ across lunar diurnal thermal swings ($-180^\circ\text{C}$ shadow to $+120^\circ\text{C}$ sunlit regolith) with zero degradation.
